Online extremism mostly relies on blaming an out-group community like women, members of the LGBTQ community, immigrants, or minorities. When an external event occurs, it is important to understand how communities respond, as it can lead to an increase or decrease in scapegoating. This post explores how we can study online extremism and economic scapegoating using a difference-in-differences design.

Difference-in-differences is a type of event study used to detect changes in behavior of a group after an event stimulus. In my research, I considered two ideologically distinct types of communities: political and apolitical forums. The political forums, including Stormfront, Vanguard, and White Nations, discuss immigration, minorities, and political grievances directed at out-group communities. The apolitical forums, including pickup artist forums, Men Going Their Own Way (MGTOW), and Incelsis, focus on relationships with women. Both types of forums have different triggers. When a positive job surprise occurred, the two communities reacted very differently. Politically charged forums showed a reduction in economic grievance posting, suggesting less scapegoating when economic conditions improve. Apolitical forums showed no significant change. Notably, before the event, both forum types recorded similar levels of economic grievance posts, a finding confirmed by pre-trend analysis, which validates that the difference-in-differences design is appropriate here.

This analysis reveals that in-group communities use external events to scapegoat out-group communities whenever their group does not personally benefit from a change. This distinction is important because it exposes the real motive behind extremist community behavior.

With this finding in mind, policymakers can better understand why extremist communities proliferate and how monitoring out-group blame patterns could help identify when communities are most likely to escalate.
